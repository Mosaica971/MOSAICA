Guadeloupe imports close to \SI{80}{\percent} of its food, and food prices there are
\SI{40}{\percent} higher than in mainland France. Sugarcane and export banana occupy most of its
farmland. The TI-FIG project, run by INRAE's ASTRO research unit, seeks to assess \emph{ex ante}
scenarios for developing local food crops; the tool intended for that purpose was MOSAICA, a
bioeconomic crop-allocation model published in 2015, which assigns each field of a territory to a
crop so as to maximise risk-adjusted gross margin under various constraints (agronomic,
land-tenure, labour, etc.). The design and the exploration of the 2015 model are detailed in the
article by \parencite{chopin2015}.

The work presented here ports MOSAICA to Python/Pyomo from GAMS. It is a mixed-integer linear
program, and the HiGHS solver handles a problem of about \num{\mesBinaires} binary variables. The
port is constrained by a parity mandate, that is, by a faithful reproduction of the GAMS model so
that future divergences in results between the two models can be attributed correctly. The model
is then calibrated against the observed 2017 land use as a reference. Once calibrated, scenarios
are explored, chosen from a grid of public policies crossed with climate and market forcings. This
exploration is completed by tracing two Pareto fronts by $\varepsilon$-constraint.

Several results are encouraging, and the scenario exploration phase already yields results:
\begin{itemize}
\item Calibration exceeds the three metrics published in the article by \parencite{chopin2015}
(\SI{\calibRetenuTypes}{\percent} of farm types reproduced,
\SI{\calibRetenuParcelles}{\percent} of fields, \SI{\calibRetenuSurface}{\percent} of area)
\item The model simulates \SI{\simRetenuCS}{\ha} of sugarcane against \SI{\obsHaCS}{\ha} observed,
without any constraint naming it.
\item The ranking of public policies depends on the forcing. Policies are assessed with robustness
and performance indicators.
\item Territorial gross margin varies inversely with the subsidy ceiling. Public support does not buy
margin but rather stability. Relaxing that constraint makes export banana disappear, sharply
reduces sugarcane and shifts about \SI{2100}{\ha} towards food crops.
\end{itemize}

The model has shortcomings, and it is important to define its domain of validity:
\begin{itemize}
\item No market outlet is modelled apart from sugarcane and export banana. The shift of agriculture
towards food crops assumes a perfectly elastic market.
\item Only 4 crops out of 11 fall below the article's deviation threshold. The residual error
concentrates on the smallest items, that is, on the crops that occupy a small area.
\item Grassland is poorly modelled because livestock is absent from the model.
\item The yield data used as input are tabulated (statistical extrapolation and declarations).
Characterising yield field by field through simulation would describe the behaviour of the field
better and would remove the dependence on estimates.
\end{itemize}
